My journey

I was told I wasn’t qualified to study computer science — wrong background, they said. So I built my first database in Excel, because Excel didn’t ask for qualifications.

The pattern showed up early. In secondary school, everyone kept asking the same question — what’s being served today? — so I pulled the week’s menu into one simple document anyone could check. Scattered information in, one trusted reference out. I’ve been shipping that same move ever since.

At university, the computer science door was closed to me — but the build instinct didn’t care. When a student association I’d joined was drowning in scattered Excel files, I built it a database system in Excel and VBA: open the file and it scans the day for you — whose birthday it is, who missed the last meeting, who needs a follow-up, who’s graduated, who’s making progress. During national service I did it again: the hospital ward I managed inventory for received supplies but couldn’t track what was used or what was left — so I built it a stock-tracking system in Excel.

My final-year research is where it clicked: I love taking a vague problem, breaking it down with data, and presenting what it actually means. My master’s sharpened that edge into a craft, and I turned it into Insight Data Lab — a consultancy helping organizations make smart decisions with their data: gather it, refine it, make meaning from it. That work is what got me noticed by Hubtel, Ghana’s leading payments and commerce company.

There the instinct compounded: from researching user behavior, to owning features across a live commerce ecosystem, to leading platform builds for executive stakeholders — a fraud monitoring portal, live intelligence dashboards, a demand model that positions delivery riders. The way of working never changed: someone describes a problem, I get into the data myself, I spec the smallest system that solves it, and I stay with the build until it’s live.

Outside work I run the consultancy, build learning tools, and work on my German — because the next chapter of this story is a bigger room.
