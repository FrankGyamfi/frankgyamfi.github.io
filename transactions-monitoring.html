Case studies / Transactions Monitoring Portal

Turning a fraud conversation into a platform analysts open every morning

1,700+ anomaly cases detected and GHS 13.9M+ in at-risk transactions flagged, across a payments ecosystem of 30M+ registered customer accounts — and counting.

RoleLed end to end — problem to live platform
ContextHubtel — fintech, Ghana
StatusLive, in daily use by ~25 analysts

The problem

Fraud across a payments ecosystem doesn’t announce itself. It hides in merchant transaction patterns, mobile-money flows and card activity — and the people responsible for catching it were finding it one complaint at a time, after a customer had already been hurt. The ask from stakeholders was a sentence, not a spec: we need to find the suspicious transactions ourselves — before the next customer gets defrauded.

My role

I owned the journey from that sentence to a production system. I interrogated the transaction data to turn “bad transactions” into precise, testable definitions; built the prototype that made the idea tangible for stakeholders; wrote the PRD and the rule specifications; and led the cross-functional build with engineers — validating every stage against production data rather than assumptions.

What the data shaped

  • Anomaly rules grounded in each merchant’s own behavioral baseline, not one-size thresholds — what’s normal for a supermarket is suspicious for a kiosk.
  • Separate rule families for merchant, mobile-money and card activity, because each channel misbehaves differently.
  • The hold-or-sweep split itself: only patterns where the risk justifies friction — like a dormant account suddenly transacting at speed — hold a live transaction for review; everything else is detected after the fact, so fraud control never strangles legitimate payments.
  • A case workflow designed with the analysts who would live in it: detection, review, escalation with evidence attached, resolution — so a flag becomes an action, not a report.

What shipped

A fraud monitoring platform that works in two layers. Live: for targeted high-risk patterns — the classic being a dormant account that suddenly wakes up and moves money at speed — the transaction is held in flight until an officer reviews and releases it, so the loss never happens. Post-transaction: the rule families sweep completed transactions for suspicious patterns, queue flagged cases with the triggering rule and evidence attached, and analysts investigate and act — blocking an initiator before they can strike again. Detection, review, escalation and resolution tracked end to end; roughly 25 fraud and risk analysts work in it daily.

Impact

  • 1,700+ anomaly cases detected
  • GHS 13.9M+ in at-risk transactions flagged across active cases
  • 30M+ registered customer accounts under protection
  • ~25 fraud/risk analysts using it daily

What I learned

Involve the end analysts even earlier. The rules were data-sound from day one; some of the case-handling flow still had to be reshaped after launch around how reviewers actually work. The data tells you what to detect — only the users tell you how a detection should become a decision.
